# 1.1.3 highlights

**The app no longer crashes on launch in a small window on macOS 27.**
Walter Cox reported (GitHub #1) that v1.1.2 on a MacBook Air quit on every
launch once a scan existed: in a window under about 560 pt tall, which is
what a 1680x1020 pt display gets by default, AppKit raised
`NSGenericException` ("more Update Constraints in Window passes than there
are views") and the app died before the treemap drew. He bisected it to the
`VSplitView` (treemap over the lower tabs) nested inside the `HSplitView`
that held the inspector, and sent the fix: the inspector now sits beside
the split view in a plain stack. His patch ships here with his authorship.
The loop only appears in builds linked against the macOS 26 SDK, which is
what the release pipeline produces, so a local `swift build` never showed
it and neither did any Mac on the project with a large display.

**The window's minimum size is now real.** The same hunt found why the
sidebar was squeezed and the inspector clipped at 900 pt and below in
v1.1.2 too: the detail's split view is AppKit-backed and never told SwiftUI
how wide it had to be, so the 720 pt floor the window advertised was a
number nothing enforced. The detail now declares its minimum (the tab row
sets it), the sidebar has a width band, and the window floor is computed
from the panes it must hold with the inspector open — 967 pt wide, 532 pt
tall with the toolbar. The window stops there instead of laying content
out past its own edge, and the reclaim-estimate label on the tab row
shortens to the bare number, then disappears, rather than wrapping to five
lines when the row is tight. A headless layout gate hosts the whole window
at that floor on every push.

**The inspector is resizable again, without the component that crashed.**
Dragging the separator between the detail and the inspector widens or
narrows it within a 240 to 280 pt band; the width is remembered. The
handle is plain SwiftUI, not a split view, so it cannot bring the macOS 27
loop back. It is also a VoiceOver-adjustable value.

**"N items could not be read" now says which, why, and what to do.** The
callout above a scan used to be a bare count with no action. It now has
Details… and Rescan buttons, and Details… opens a list of the recorded
paths grouped by cause: protected by macOS (a missing Full Disk Access
grant, another user's files, or an app's sandbox), changed or vanished
while the scan passed (a running app's temporary files, harmless), timed
out (a cloud or network location that did not answer; its placeholders
take no local space), or failed for another reason (the exact macOS text
on hover, and repeated read errors on a local disk are worth a Disk
Utility check). Each group carries one sentence of next steps, each path
can be revealed in Finder or copied, and the Open System Settings button
appears only when Phantom has proven the grant is missing. The scan
records a sample of the first 100 such paths with the operating system's
reason; the list says so when the count is larger.

**Upgrading.** No schema change and no wire change: a 1.1.2 database opens
as it is, and every API response, MCP tool and CLI output keeps its 1.1.2
shape; the plugin manifests move to 1.1.3 for alignment only. Two visible
differences: the window cannot be made narrower than 967 pt (a saved
narrower frame grows on first launch), and the inspector opens at its
widest until you drag it. The installed app updates through Sparkle
(Phantom ▸ Check for Updates…) or `brew upgrade --cask phantom` once the
public repository is recut.
